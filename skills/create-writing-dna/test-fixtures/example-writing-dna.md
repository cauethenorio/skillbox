# Writing DNA — Testuser

One person, all languages. Built from a 3-sample fixture corpus (blog/chat/email).

## 1. Banned LLM-isms (personalized)

### English
- Verbs: delve, leverage, underscore, showcase, harness, foster.
- Adjectives (cluster tell): robust, seamless, comprehensive, pivotal.
- Phrases: "it's important to note", "at its core", "in today's fast-paced world", "the bottom line is".

### Portuguese
- Verbs/words: mergulhar, potencializar, robusto, abrangente.
- Phrases: "vale ressaltar", "é importante notar", "no mundo de hoje".

### Em-dash rule
No em-dashes. Use a period or a comma, or restructure. Never substitute a colon.

## 2. Anti-performative rules
- Don't turn the dry one-liner into a catchphrase. One per piece, max.
- Don't announce the point before making it. Just make it.

## 3. Voice essence
Direct and dry. Short sentences, concrete numbers, a deadpan closing line. Trusts the reader; never lectures.

## 4. Core voice patterns
- Sentence structure: short, declarative; fragments for emphasis. Seen in 3/3 samples.
- Vocabulary fingerprint: plain words; leaves technical terms in English even in Portuguese ("deploy", "merge", "build"). Seen in 1/1 chat sample.
- Opening patterns: jumps straight in; no scene-setting. Seen in 3/3 samples.
- Punctuation quirks: no em-dashes; ends on a short punchy line. Seen in 2/3 samples.

## 5. Language-specific patterns

### English voice
Professional but blunt. One pleasantry max in email, then straight to the point.

### Portuguese voice
Casual, lowercase, rapid-fire in chat. Light emoji (🙃). Informal "a gente".

### Code-switching rules
Technical nouns stay in English inside Portuguese sentences (deploy, merge, build, force push, main). Everything else in Portuguese.

## 6. Channel modes
- Default: informational. State the thing, stop.
- Blog: narrative OK; open with a concrete incident, close with a one-line rule.
- Chat/Slack: rapid-fire short lines, lowercase, minimal punctuation, occasional emoji.
- Email: one pleasantry, numbered points, sign off with first name only.

## 7. Drafted vs Sent
- Drafted: "I wanted to reach out to flag that it's important to note the deploy may have introduced some instability."
  Sent: "the deploy passed. what broke was the merge after."
  Lesson: cut the hedging preamble; state what actually happened.
